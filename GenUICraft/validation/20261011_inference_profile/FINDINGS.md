# E2B inference bottlenecks — final code and device diagnosis

The trained E2B v10 corrected-FP16 GPU profile completed **8/8 arms, 36 measured conversions, six separately excluded startup warmups, and 12/12 saved-document display checks** across Fold8 (SM-F971B, `R37L20059SY`) and Flip8 (SM-F776U, `R3GL203AKSF`). All 36 measured conversions completed, saved final A2UI, and reported the expected native cold/warm engine state; that establishes execution, **not semantic accuracy**. Both phones report SM8850, but Fold8 has an `eng` build and Flip8 a `userdebug` build, so their absolute times are presented separately. The frozen corpus, corrected model, and prompt hashes are recorded in [provenance](provenance.json). This is a headless model probe plus a separate replay of saved outputs, not a tap-to-ready benchmark of the live SDK page. No production code was changed.

The source data and full per-request flags are in [REPORT.md](REPORT.md), [per_request.csv](per_request.csv), and [summary.json](summary.json). The figures show **observed** intervals and throughput; they are not causal speedup estimates.

## Clock boundaries before interpreting the numbers

`conversionWallMs` starts just before the probe observer is constructed and ends after `GenUiSession.convert()` returns. It includes provider work, final compile/repair, and SDK preview work, but the probe deliberately defers MTP metrics finalization. `providerTotalMs` sits **inside** that wall interval; engine initialization sits inside the provider interval. Native prefill/decode durations are derived from token counts divided by native rates. Native TTFT includes prefill, while LiteRT's reported initialization *phase sum* can overlap native phases; neither can be added to the wall interval. The probe separately records construction, `finishGenerationMetrics()`, and `closeAndAwait()` (`InferenceBottleneckProbeTest.kt:125-173,211-305,308-334`; `GenUiSession.kt:127-168`; `Api.kt:63-80`). Adding cold conversion and teardown intervals is an **analytical sum with intervening artifact writes**, not a directly observed user tap latency. Full boundaries and overlap are in [CODE_AUDIT.md](CODE_AUDIT.md).

## Cold train case: initialization consumes much of the request

All four measured cold BXP-003 requests produced the same 428-token raw output. Seconds below are individual requests in order; prefill/decode are derived estimates, and metrics finish is a **separate** later interval.

| Device | Cold request | Convert wall | Native engine-init wall | Prefill* | Decode* | Metrics finish after convert | Thermal start→end |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Fold8 | 1 | 29.20 | 18.78 | 3.63 | 6.60 | 1.86 | 0→1 |
| Fold8 | 2 | 16.82 | 7.09 | 3.38 | 6.27 | 2.40 | 0→2 |
| Flip8 | 1 | 19.27 | 10.42 | 2.38 | 6.36 | 0.66 | 0→0 |
| Flip8 | 2 | 12.57 | 4.82 | 1.66 | 6.02 | 0.71 | 0→0 |

In these **four baseline requests only**, initialization occupies about **38–64%** of conversion wall. Their post-init decode rates are 64.89/68.30 tokens/s on Fold8 and 67.30/71.06 on Flip8. Fold8's baseline ended thermally elevated, so it fails the strict-cooled gate; Flip8's two cold requests pass it. For the same BXP-003 output with native-warm MTP-on engine reuse, Fold8 conversion was 7.94/7.28 s and Flip8 7.21/7.10 s, with zero engine-init time in each. These are meaningful within-device observations, but not a randomized, equally cooled cold/warm effect size. Sources: [per_request.csv](per_request.csv); `Gemma4Runtime.kt:96-105,260-325`.

The **six excluded startup warmups** are real cold starts before each reused-provider arm. They are absent from arm medians and MTP/streaming pairs. Their conversion/init seconds were Fold8 MTP-on **23.55/16.84**, MTP-off **53.12/34.99**, streaming-on **45.19/35.08**; Flip8 MTP-off **22.26/14.36**, MTP-on **25.93/20.83**, streaming-on **15.25/10.16**. Fold8's 53.12 s warmup began and ended at thermal status 4 after a prior hot arm. Consequently, **4.82–18.78 s is the baseline-init range, not the full observed cold-init range**; the six warmups reached 35.08 s. No cooldown gate preceded these warmups, so they should not be pooled into the measured comparisons. Evidence: [REPORT.md, “Excluded startup warmups”](REPORT.md), arm `device/summary.json` files.

The BXP-001 final A2UI matches across modes and emits the same 214 tokens, but its raw text differs. On Flip8, resource samples for the first BXP-001 in both modes show a **1300 MHz reported GPU maximum**, while MTP-off decoded at 40.6 tokens/s and MTP-on at 76.4 tokens/s. That is stronger mode-associated evidence than the long-case wall gap, even though order, coarse thermal state, and unsampled conditions still differ. For Flip8's MTP-off BXP-031, native decode alone occupied **52.76/45.63 s** of **54.81/47.60 s** conversion. Its sampled reported GPU maximum fell to **342 MHz** at roughly 89% busy, while MTP-on long requests sampled 1300 MHz; the long-case gap cannot be assigned entirely to MTP. On Fold8, repeated MTP-on BXP-031 had identical 886-token raw output but rate fell **74.31→42.09 tokens/s** and wall rose **14.21→25.67 s**; the later run coincided with a **461 MHz reported GPU ceiling**, thermal 2→4, and elevated memory/CPU PSI. Those observations narrow the bottleneck to sustained decode plus changing device conditions, without identifying a particular kernel or the policy that set the clock ceiling. See [RESOURCE_ANALYSIS.md](RESOURCE_ANALYSIS.md), [NATIVE_ANALYSIS.md](NATIVE_ANALYSIS.md), and [per_request.csv](per_request.csv).

The MTP-on engine-session acceptance values (86.60% for each six-case warm control; 81.64% for each four-case preview control) come from engine destruction. **Each includes the excluded cold warmup as well as measured warm requests**; they are neither per-request acceptance nor evidence of a specific speedup. The MTP-off modes have no acceptance value. The MTP-on/off arms have only three strictly cooled requests across all 36 measured requests, none forming a strictly cooled MTP pair. All reported mode differences retain run-order, thermal, and output caveats ([REPORT.md](REPORT.md); `Gemma4Runtime.kt:197-225`).

## Preview and display are not the dominant measured model interval

The native response and final output hashes matched exactly between paired preview-off and preview-on cases. On Flip8, the four warm MTP-on preview-on conversions differed from preview-off by **0.00, 0.00, +0.07, and approximately 0.00 s**; on Fold8 the four differences were **+0.70, −0.02, −0.06, +0.35 s** under different hot conditions. First ready **SDK preview callbacks** arrived **2.53–3.03 s** on Fold8 and **2.46–3.57 s** on Flip8 from headless conversion start. These are neither first-painted-pixel nor final-document times. The switch disables preview compilation/publication, while native async raw streaming and repetition checking remain active (`GenUiSession.kt:122-145`; `Gemma4Runtime.kt:157-164`; `GenUiPreviewCoordinator.kt:20-57`). Provider boundary residuals remain small, but native callback work is *inside* provider time; the probe's `callbackBodyWorkMs` is only its tiny observer body (`GenUiStreamingProvider.kt:25-40`; `Gemma4Streaming.kt:29-51`). There is no evidence here for a major preview-path wall penalty or for declaring JNI callback cost negligible.

In a **separate saved-output replay without model inference**, the render probe compiled and passed **6/6 checks on each phone**; the unique status label became accessibility-visible in all 12 cases. Fold8 compiler time ranged **3.06–17.18 ms** (median **8.22 ms**) and label observation **193.61–567.95 ms** (median **229.33 ms**). Flip8 compiler time ranged **4.18–17.95 ms** (median **8.58 ms**) and label observation **241.75–605.34 ms** (median **296.78 ms**). The status observation is a polling **upper bound for accessibility-visible state**, not a frame-present/pixel-paint timestamp. Screenshots were captured *after* the measured interval; their weather, train, and checklist views were visually checked on both phones. This replay does not include native generation or a guaranteed process-cold Activity launch. Sources: `InferenceRenderTimingProbeTest.kt:18-25,83-164`; [Fold8 render results](R37L20059SY/render_timing/device/results.json), [Flip8 render results](R3GL203AKSF/render_timing/device/results.json), and paired screenshot files in those directories. The first Flip8 render test failed an identity-only assertion; the preserved `render_timing_initial_probe_failed` artifact records it. The corrected test compares document value at `InferenceRenderTimingProbeTest.kt:114-119`; the final 12/12 checks use that correction.

## Ranked practical changes

1. **Retain the trained provider/engine across compatible requests.** This removes repeated weight/graph setup from the *warm request path*, the largest confirmed avoidable interval in the cold baseline. The SDK demo already retains a provider by settings key (`GenUiSdkDemoViewModel.kt:141-174`), but the Stage 3 bridge creates and closes a fresh session/provider each time (`TrainedStage3Bridge.kt:135-144,176-209`). Add an owned lifecycle with serialization, cancellation, model/settings invalidation, idle release, and memory limits. The corrected-FP16 model hash check is once per file identity per process (`GpuFp16Correction.kt:74-88`). A persistent shader-cache experiment is separate: its current PID-scoped directory is intentional for corrected-patch validation (`Gemma4Runtime.kt:285-293`), so do not remove the PID or reuse serialized contexts without proving the correction still applies. Native traces confirm GPU graph delegation but have no per-kernel/weight-loading time split ([NATIVE_ANALYSIS.md](NATIVE_ANALYSIS.md)).

2. **Keep close-only MTP metrics off the interactive completion path.** With metrics and MTP enabled, `GenUiSession.convert()` finalizes before returning, and `Gemma4Runtime.finishGenerationMetrics()` closes the engine to read session-level acceptance (`GenUiSession.kt:153-168`; `Gemma4Runtime.kt:197-225`). That defeats the demo's retained-provider reuse and places finalization on the user completion path. Defer acceptance collection until engine retirement, or separate live token counters from close-only acceptance. As an **immediate SDK-demo-only workaround**, `enableMetrics=false` lets its retained provider remain warm across matching settings, but loses native benchmark counters/acceptance and does **not** fix the fresh Stage 3 bridge session. The demo defaults metrics and MTP on, subject to persisted preferences and selected-model support (`GenUiSdkDemoActivity.kt:63,91,105-106,652-663`). Verify the live settings before attributing a particular UI run to this mechanism.

3. **Prewarm asynchronously while upstream work proceeds.** `Gemma4Runtime` initializes the engine lazily on first generation (`Gemma4Runtime.kt:96-105`), so a library-owned prepare API could start correct model validation/engine creation during earlier Stage 1/2 fetching, before Stage 3 needs it. This can **hide** startup from the Stage 3 critical path, but does not reduce initialization work, power, memory use, or the six observed cold warmups. It needs cancellation and thermal/memory admission control. A dummy generation is not a substitute for a measured, explicit preparation API.

4. **Treat output tokens and repeated prompt prefill as separate costs.** Long warm requests are decode-dominated; preserve MTP where supported and validate output quality under paired controls, without claiming an exact causal gain from these thermally unbalanced runs. BXP-003 has **3394 input tokens**, and warm prefill still takes roughly **1.56–2.12 s**. The trained profile uses 8192 context, 2048 output, no thinking (`GenUiSession.kt:25-50`), and a pinned three-message prompt (`GenUiTrainedConverter.kt:167-193`). `Gemma4Runtime` creates and closes a new conversation for each request (`:130-132,190-193`), so keeping the engine does **not** reuse prompt KV. Prefix-KV clone/cache would require verified native API support or an extension in this LiteRT-LM 0.16.1 integration; the engine `cacheDir` is not a prompt-KV cache. Any prompt compression or tighter output policy must be checked against the checkpoint contract and semantic quality, not inferred from repair kind `NONE`.

5. **Measure and manage thermal/memory pressure before lower-level tuning.** Preserve per-request status, battery temperature, reported GPU clock ceiling, busy samples, run order, and memory PSI. The 342/461 MHz ceilings coincide with severe long-request slowdowns, but their cause is not measured. Cooldown timeout is nonfatal in the probe (`InferenceBottleneckProbeTest.kt:139-165,174-185,336-381`), and only **3/36** measured requests passed the strict-cooled gate. Retry contested comparisons from a verified cool state and monitor sustained production sessions. This evidence does not justify a kernel rewrite, bandwidth-bound claim, FLOP/GFLOPS estimate, or clock override.

The recommended next verification after any lifecycle change is a real SDK-page button-to-first-painted-content and button-to-ready capture, with the current model/toggle state recorded. Keep it distinct from headless `conversionWallMs`, separately timed MTP teardown, and saved-output render replay.
