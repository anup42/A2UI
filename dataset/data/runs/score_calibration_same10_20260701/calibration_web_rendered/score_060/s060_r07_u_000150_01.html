That’s soul-pop, indie pop, jazz-rap, synth-pop, minimalist alt-pop, disco-pop, rap, dreamy alt-pop, country-folk, and soul-rock—all in the “popular music after 2010” bucket.

I do think your point is partly fair: streaming, playlist culture, and certain production tools have made some songs converge around familiar beats, vocal effects, and structures. So yes, there are trends.

But trends aren’t the same thing as sameness. It’s more accurate to say popular music often shares a few surface features while still being wildly different in rhythm, vocal style, emotion, instrumentation, and genre roots.

A better test might be: if these songs really “all sound the same,” could you honestly confuse “Rolling in the Deep” with “Alright,” or “bad guy” with “Something in the Orange”? Probably not.
