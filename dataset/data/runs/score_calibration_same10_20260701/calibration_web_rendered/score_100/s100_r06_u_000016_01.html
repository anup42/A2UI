Damp Patch Triage for a Lisbon Ground-Floor Flat

Initial assessment

You likely have water ingress or moisture migration, not a paint problem. The strongest clues are ground-floor location, near an exterior corner, worse after heavy rain, and repainting failed twice.

Spend the budget on finding the cause and doing one targeted repair, not on cosmetic replastering alone.

Quote says treat damp wall but not where water enters

Wants to replaster or paint before verifying drying period

Evidence to Gather Before Requesting Quotes

Prepare one compact evidence pack and send the same pack to every professional so quotes are more comparable.

Photo set of the wall: full wall, close-up of patch, exterior corner outside the affected area, and ceiling or wall junction plus skirting area.

Measurements: patch width about 1.2 m, height from floor to highest visible mark, and distance from exterior corner.

Rain pattern note: gets worse after heavy rain; note how soon it appears after rain, same day or next day.

Chronology: repainted twice, problem returned, and no burst pipes found.

Building context: ground-floor flat, interior wall near exterior corner, and approximate flat size 70 m².

Simple moisture clues: bubbling paint, flaking plaster, tide marks near skirting, mold smell, white salts or powder, and surface cold to touch.

Weather correlation log: 2-4 dates of heavy rain and whether patch expanded, plus a plan sketch showing affected wall and exterior side.

Smart extras if you can gather them

Infrared photos, if available from a surveyor or leak technician

Condominium or building records of facade repairs

Photos of gutters, downpipes, or exterior cracks near that corner

Humidity readings in the room over several days

How to Shortlist and Compare Bids

When contacting firms, prefer those who will do these things in writing.

State the probable source before proposing materials

Distinguish between diagnosis, repair, and internal finish

Price each separately

Confirm whether they need exterior access, ladder, or condominium approval

Explain whether repair is exterior-first, interior-only temporary, or both sides

Say how long to wait before repainting

Questions every quote should answer

What is the most likely cause and what are the second and third possibilities?

What evidence supports that view?

Is the repair aimed at the entry point or only the damaged interior surface?

Will you inspect the outside face of the wall?

What moisture testing tools will you use?

If the first repair fails, what is the next diagnostic step?

What warranty applies, and does it cover recurrence after rain?

What parts of the job need condominium or building permission?

Lisbon-Specific Notes

In Lisbon flats, recurring damp near an exterior corner is often linked to aging render or facade cracks.

Failed sealants around joints or openings can allow water entry from shared building elements.

Limited ventilation can make a rain-ingress problem look worse indoors.

If the exterior wall or facade is a common building element, repair responsibility may partly involve the condominium rather than only your flat.

Ask any surveyor to note this in the report.
