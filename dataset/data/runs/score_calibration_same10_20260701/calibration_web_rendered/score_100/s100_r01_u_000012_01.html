Most Likely Causes Ranked

These are ranked by fit to your symptoms and by how often they can be fixed without blowing past the budget.

Ignition misfire from worn plugs or weak coil

Rank1
CauseIgnition misfire from worn plugs or weak coil
DetailsBest fit if the CEL is tied to P030x codes. A mild misfire can show up most at idle or stoplights and crush city MPG. At 92,000 miles, spark plugs are a natural checkpoint even if not yet catastrophic.
Budget Range$120-$300 for spark plugs; $120-$250 for one coil; full coil set if truly needed often pushes total higher, so diagnose first

Dirty throttle body / intake carbon

Rank2
CauseDirty throttle body / intake carbon
DetailsVery common on short-trip city driving. Can cause unstable idle without hard starting or smoke. May also worsen fuel economy if idle control is compensating.
Budget Range$100-$250

Vacuum leak or PCV hose issue

Rank3
CauseVacuum leak or PCV hose issue
DetailsCauses rough idle because leaks matter most at idle. Often triggers lean codes like P0171 and elevated fuel trims. Can be inexpensive if it is just a hose, seal, or PCV-related part.
Budget Range$50-$250

Upstream O2 / air-fuel sensor or MAP/MAF reading issue

Rank4
CauseUpstream O2 / air-fuel sensor or MAP/MAF reading issue
DetailsA bad sensor can enrich the mixture and drop MPG fast. Less likely than ignition or vacuum issues unless code data points there.
Budget Range$180-$450

Thermostat stuck open

Rank5
CauseThermostat stuck open
DetailsEspecially plausible with short trips and a noticeable MPG drop. Engine may stay cooler than intended, running richer longer. Usually does not cause severe rough idle by itself, so this may be secondary.
Budget Range$180-$350

Injector or purge valve issue

Rank6
CauseInjector or purge valve issue
DetailsInjector imbalance can cause rough idle and fuel loss. A purge valve stuck open can create rough idle after starts and at stops. Diagnose after cheaper common items are checked.
Budget Range$150-$500

Best Repair Path Under $900

For your budget, the smartest order is:

1. Get the codes read first. Many parts stores do this free, but a shop with live data is better.

2. If codes show misfire, start with inspect plugs, swap or test suspect coil, and replace only failed coil parts unless plugs are overdue.

3. If fuel trims show lean, check intake leaks, PCV or vacuum hoses, and purge valve behavior.

4. If idle is rough but ignition and vacuum checks are clean, clean throttle body and perform idle relearn.

5. If MPG remains poor and live data points there, test upstream O2 or A/F sensor and verify coolant temperature and thermostat operation.

Practical urgency rule:

If the check-engine light is flashing, stop driving except to a repair shop.

If the check-engine light is steady, keep driving light and short only until codes are checked.
