Winner, Runner-Up, and Caution Pick

Winner: Interpreter of Maladies

Why it wins

• Reading load is light at under 200 pages in most editions.

• The stories invite lively discussion on marriage, identity, migration, intimacy, loneliness, and miscommunication.

• It is literary without being punishing; most readers can engage deeply without needing heavy prep.

• The tone is serious but not relentlessly bleak; there is emotional warmth and subtle humor alongside sadness.

Why it suits your group

• Works well for mixed reading preferences in a 6-person club.

• Gives plenty to discuss story by story, so everyone can latch onto at least one favorite.

• Feels substantial without becoming a chore for a one-month read.

Runner-up: Tenth of December

Why it nearly wins

• Excellent for animated conversation: morality, class, loneliness, kindness, modern absurdity.

• More emotionally varied than its reputation suggests; it can be dark, but it is also compassionate and funny.

• The page count is comfortably manageable.

Why it finishes second

• Its voice and stylistic shifts can be a bit more demanding for some readers than Lahiri’s collection.

• A few stories may split the group more sharply on accessibility.

Caution Pick: What We Talk About When We Talk About Love

Why to be cautious

• It easily fits the page limit and is highly discussable.

• But it is also the option most likely to feel too stark and emotionally chilly for a group explicitly avoiding relentlessly bleak reads.

• Minimalism can produce great debate, but it may leave some readers admiring it more than enjoying it.

Why the Others Rank Lower

• Exhalation: Brilliant and discussion-rich, but well over your preferred page limit in most editions and a bit more conceptually demanding.

• Dubliners: Strong symbolic fit for a Dublin club and within range, but its emotional climate is often subdued and somber rather than lively.

• Her Body and Other Parties: Possibly the most explosive discussion book here, but also the riskiest on tone and difficulty for this exact brief.
