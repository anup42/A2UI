Host Script Outline

Opening welcome: 18:40

Good evening everyone, and welcome. We’re really glad you’re here. Tonight is about sharing a little bit of who we are, learning something new about one another, and enjoying a relaxed cultural evening together.

Some of us grew up in Dublin, some arrived recently, and many of us probably carry more than one culture with us. Tonight, there’s no pressure to perform perfectly or know everything. Curiosity is enough.

Ground-setting

A quick note before we begin: this is a friendly, low-pressure evening. You’re welcome to join in fully, quietly observe, or take part when something suits you. All are welcome.

Transition into first activity

To start simply, we’ll begin with something everyone already has: a greeting.

Before the showcase

This next part is informal and brief. If you’d like to share a line, a proverb, a phrase, a memory, or even a short tune, this is your moment. It’s about offering something small and meaningful.

Finale setup

To close, we’ll create something together—a simple community mosaic made from many small pieces, which feels very fitting for tonight.

Closing words

Thank you for the openness, humour, and kindness you brought into the room tonight. We hope you leave having learned a few new names, a few new words, and maybe a new story to carry with you.

Five Audience-Friendly Segments

Segment 1: World Hello Circle

SegmentSegment 1: World Hello Circle
DetailsInvite volunteers or pre-selected attendees to teach a greeting in their language.
Timing / StructureAim for 6-8 greetings total to keep pace lively.
Why it worksInstantly inclusive; no specialist talent required; lets many cultures appear early in the event

Segment 2: Tabletop Culture Quiz

SegmentSegment 2: Tabletop Culture Quiz
DetailsDivide guests into tables of 5-6 people and run 3 short rounds.
Timing / StructureFood origins; festivals and traditions; famous places, music, or sayings
Why it worksKeep questions light and surprising rather than academic; give small low-cost prizes like chocolates, tea bags, or bookmarks

Segment 3: Objects and Stories

SegmentSegment 3: Objects and Stories
DetailsPre-arrange 4-6 participants to bring a small object, photo, recipe card, scarf, or memory item.
Timing / StructureEach gets 2 minutes: what it is, why it matters, what it says about home, family, or tradition
Why it worksPersonal without becoming heavy; gives emotional depth to the evening; easy indoors, no tech dependence

Segment 4: Common Ground Mixer

SegmentSegment 4: Common Ground Mixer
DetailsPlace signs around the room with prompts and let guests move to a prompt that interests them.
Timing / StructureChat for 4 minutes and rotate once or twice
Why it worksAvoids awkward forced mingling; encourages cross-cultural conversation; works well for mixed ages and confidence levels

Segment 5: Mini Showcase — Words, Lines, or Tunes

SegmentSegment 5: Mini Showcase — Words, Lines, or Tunes
DetailsInvite 5-8 short contributions, each under 90 seconds.
Timing / StructureSuitable contributions include a proverb, poem excerpt, blessing, folk saying, short song line, or childhood rhyme
Why it worksKeep it intentionally brief to protect pacing
