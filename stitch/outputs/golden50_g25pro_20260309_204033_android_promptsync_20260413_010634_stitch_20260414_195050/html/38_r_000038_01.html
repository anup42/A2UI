The Earth's Cracked Shell

An Introduction to Plate Tectonics. Imagine the Earth as a giant, hard-boiled egg with a shell that has been cracked. These massive puzzle pieces are the Lithospheric Plates.

How the Earth's Plates Interact

The lithosphere is divided into about a dozen major and many minor plates. These plates move relative to one another, driven by convection currents in the underlying mantle. This constant motion shapes our world, creating mountains, oceans, and defining the very ground we stand on.

CONVERGENT

The Collision Course

When two plates move toward each other, they collide. This process, known as subduction or continental collision, created the majestic Himalayas as the Indian Plate pushed into the Eurasian Plate.

"The crust is essentially folded and pushed upwards, forming some of the highest peaks on Earth."

DIVERGENT

The Big Split

At divergent boundaries, plates pull apart. As the rift widens, molten rock (magma) rises from the mantle to fill the gap, creating new lithospheric crust. The Mid-Atlantic Ridge is a prime example of this "seafloor spreading."

TRANSFORM

The Side-Swipe

Plates slide past each other horizontally. These boundaries don't usually create mountains or oceans, but they do generate massive amounts of stress. When this stress is suddenly released, it causes earthquakes, like those along California's San Andreas Fault.
